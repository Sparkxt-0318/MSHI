### Decisions

1. **Log and scripts.** No earlier log or scripts were available; this log starts here; scripts
   20-36 with shared code in `audit/mshi_common.py` and `audit/mshi_sites.py`.
2. **SRDB reading.** `keep_default_na=False, na_values=[""]`, so "None" survives; unmanipulated =
   `Manipulation == "None"` or missing.
3. **Quality flags.** Parsed into sets after normalising spellings ("Q1" -> Q01, "Q01, Q03",
   "Q03,Q10,Q16"); dropped if any of Q10-Q14. Q15 and Q16 kept (open question 1).
4. **Known duplicates (added rule).** `Duplicate_record` set -> dropped (13 after the earlier rules;
   64 among records with Rs_annual), because duplicated values would shrink within-cell variance.
5. **COSORE vs SRDB duplicates (added after review).** A COSORE dataset-year is dropped when an SRDB
   record lies in the same 0.05-degree cell with an overlapping year (21 of 63; e.g. Haibei, El
   Eden, Yanchi, Laegeren): several COSORE datasets are the raw data behind SRDB entries. COSORE
   datasets from one submission (first two name tokens, e.g. d20200212_KAYE_*) share one study id.
6. **Annual coverage.** Coverage < 1 dropped. Coverage missing (368 SRDB records) kept in variant
   `incl_missing`, dropped in `strict`. Every analysis was run on both; `strict` is primary.
7. **Continents.** Natural Earth map units (which split French Guiana, Réunion, Puerto Rico from
   their parent countries), CONTINENT field, UN subregions Central America + Caribbean + South
   America grouped as "South & Central America" (Mexico included). Overrides: Russia east of 60 E
   or west of 0 (Chukotka) -> Asia; Hawaii -> Oceania; Canary Islands -> Africa. Points within 10 km
   of a land unit take that unit. No cell had records in two continents.
8. **Country check.** Agreement = point inside or within 10 km of a unit carrying the stated name
   (sovereign names count, so "Denmark" in Greenland agrees). Distances are exact geodesic for
   polygons within 300 km (local azimuthal-equidistant projection; added after review, because the
   planar nearest point overstated high-latitude distances, e.g. 17.9 vs 9.8 km at Kannus, Finland).
   "Political" equivalence (Ukraine/Russia, Serbia/Kosovo, China/Taiwan, India/Pakistan/China, ...)
   only within 50 km of the stated country, inside Crimea, or inside a disputed/partially
   recognised unit (added after review: "India" records 95 km inside Pakistan had been accepted).
   Every disagreement beyond 10 km is flagged and excluded from modelling, including the
   low-severity near-border and near-offshore classes (open question 3).
9. **Cells.** MODIS-CMG-aligned 0.05-degree cells (floor binning from 180 W / 90 N), unlike
   `src/build_target.py` (round(), one arbitrary record). Site = mean of log Rs over all records in
   the cell; `n_records`, `sd_log_rs`, `n_studies` kept.
10. **WorldClim sampling.** Nearest 30" pixel; if nodata, nearest valid pixel within 3 px (~2.7 km;
    12 records); 8 records with no value within 3 px dropped. Site predictors = mean over the
    unique record locations in the cell.
11. **COSORE annualisation (rewritten).** Only `CSR_MSMT_VAR == "Rs"` and `CSR_TREATMENT == "None"`
    ports (the old `scripts/cosore_aggregate.py` also averaged trenched, girdled, warmed and
    irrigated ports and NEE/Reco chambers). One record per dataset x calendar year; a port-year
    counts only if all 12 months have >= 5 days of data (analogue of SRDB coverage = 1); fluxes
    outside [-2, 50] umol m-2 s-1 dropped; annual Rs = day-weighted mean of monthly means x 12.011 x
    seconds / 1e6. rdata 0.11.2 reads 85 of 91 datasets; the 6 others have no `data.RDS` in the
    repository (not a parse failure). A repeated port number in d20190821_LIU/PORTS.txt is mapped to
    the one data port missing from the file (port 6; added after review). Result: 63 dataset-years
    from 28 datasets (>= 1 day per month would add one dataset).
12. **Analysis 1 design.** Train continents need >= 150 sites, test continents >= 20. XGBoost grid:
    max_depth {1,2,3,4,6} x learning_rate {0.03,0.1} x min_child_weight {1,5,10} x reg_lambda {1,10},
    subsample = colsample = 0.8, n_estimators {5,10,20,30,50,100,150,300,600} read off one 600-tree
    fit with `iteration_range`. The first grid had to be widened twice: once before any result was
    recorded (depth >= 2 / >= 150 trees put the optimum on the edge), and once after review (5-30
    trees, because Europe's optimum sat on the 50-tree corner and was worse than a mean-only model in
    block CV). Selection: lowest pooled out-of-fold MSE under 5-fold 5-degree spatial-block CV in the
    training continent; the test continent never enters tuning. Within-continent skill: 5-fold
    5-degree block CV with the tuning repeated inside each outer training set. R2 on log Rs with
    test-set SST; RMSE, MAE, bias in g C m-2 yr-1 after exp() (no smearing); Spearman.
13. **Dissimilarity index.** Meyer & Pebesma (2021) DI with predictors standardised by the training
    continent and weighted by each variable's share of XGBoost total gain (as caret/CAST does);
    the first run used average gain per split, which a reviewer found departs from CAST (kept as a
    sensitivity column). AOA threshold = upper whisker of block-CV training DIs.
14. **MODIS NPP run.** XGBoost with and without NPP is compared at matched hyperparameters (after
    review: separately tuned models had confounded the NPP effect with a change of
    hyperparameters).
15. **Analysis 2 noise model.** Records in the same cell and study agree twice as well as records
    from different studies, and most cells hold one study, so the primary ceiling (B2) uses a nested
    cell -> study -> record model. The one-way pooled ceiling (B1) is reported as an optimistic upper
    value; B3 = 1 - semivariance(0-10 km)/variance for predictors smooth beyond 10 km. Ceiling
    intervals are percentiles of the bound itself, with the test continent's sites resampled (after
    review: holding Var(site means) fixed understated the uncertainty for Africa and Oceania).
    "Same year" = overlapping 12-month windows (|delta mid-year| <= 0.5), not exact equality (after
    review); exact equality kept as a sensitivity. Exponential variogram fits are restricted to lags
    <= 1600 km (fits to all lags ran to the range bound because of the continental-scale trend).
16. **Analysis 3 classification.** Order: Region names the admin-1 unit the point is in -> country
    label wrong; sign flip or lat/lon swap lands in the stated country -> coordinate fix (if the
    original point is within 50 km of the stated country, only with the Region naming a province
    within ~25 km of the fixed point; added after review: four "Spain" records 25 km inside France
    had been moved 400 km west); point at sea; near border; title/Region text names the actual
    country -> label wrong; Region names a province of the stated country -> coordinates wrong; else
    unclear. Same-site rule: if other records of the same study and site name lie in the stated
    country and agree within 0.1 deg, their coordinates are the fix. Species names containing
    countries ("Norway spruce", "Turkey oak") are stripped before text matching. "Corrected" policy =
    high- and medium-confidence fixes applied, the rest excluded.
17. **XGBoost selection instability.** Re-tuning on data sets that differ by a few sites can change
    the selected configuration and move transfer R2 by up to 0.17, so Analysis 3 also reports
    XGBoost with hyperparameters fixed at the `excluded` values, `31_a1_tuning_sensitivity.py`
    reports transfer R2 across the 10 best configurations, and `36_a1_foldseed_sensitivity.py`
    repeats the within-continent block CV over fold seeds 42-46.
18. **Figures.** Reference palette slots 1-3 (blue, orange, aqua) validated with the dataviz
    validator on white for all pairs (worst CVD dE 9.2); the same colour always means the same
    training continent; R2 heat maps use a diverging blue-grey-red ramp with matched OKLab
    lightness per arm, saturating at +-0.5 (cell text gives the real value).

Search modes: WebSearch, Crossref search, Europe PMC full-text search (about 400 open-access hits,
323 full texts machine-scanned for A1), full-text reads where hosts allowed (ESSD, OSTI accepted
manuscripts, PMC), SRDB git history and GitHub issue search. No Web of Science or Google Scholar;
OpenAlex unavailable. Every DOI cited here was checked against Crossref by
`29_crossref_check.py` (63 of 63 ok). After the two search workflows, one adversarial agent per
analysis tried to refute the A1 and A2 novelty claims (79 and 75 searches; their WebSearch budget ran
out before two planned queries each). Papers were read by those agents, not by me, except where a
statement below says the abstract was checked in Crossref. "Abstract only" claims are
[UNVERIFIED] beyond the abstract.

- **A1.** No soil-respiration (Rs or Rh) upscaling study found that holds out a continent or large
  region or reports a transfer matrix. Where methods were readable, validation is random k-fold
  (Tang 2020, Huang 2020, Liu J 2024, Sun 2023), leave-one-out (Ciais 2021), leave-one-year-out and
  leave-one-site-out (Laffitte 2026 Nat Commun) or site-grouped folds (Meng 2026). Unreadable
  methods: Lu 2021, Stell 2021, Warner 2019, Yao 2021, Jiang 2024, Zhao 2024 (abstract says 10-fold)
  [UNVERIFIED]. He 2022 reportedly stratified 10-fold CV by climate zone (search snippet only)
  [UNVERIFIED]. Adjacent: FLUXCOM uses whole-site folds without continent hold-out (Tramontana 2016,
  Jung 2020, Nelson 2024, read in full via OSTI); Papale 2015 found European-trained networks
  degrade in the tropics (abstract); Liu L 2024 used spatially grouped hold-outs for ecosystem
  respiration in the US Midwest; Malhotra 2026 assessed SRDB's representativeness in bioclimatic
  space. The idea itself is established outside Rs (Ploton 2020; Meyer & Pebesma 2021, 2022 -
  which explicitly asks for accuracy by continent; Roberts 2017; Ludwig 2023).
  Refuter: the claim survives for continents, but "large region" must be narrowed to "continent or
  comparably large multi-country region". About 94 more Europe PMC full texts were scanned with no
  hits. The closest verified analogue is Liu L 2024 (full text): spatial groups of towers and one
  US state held out, but for tower Reco/NEE and yield inside the US Midwest, not soil Rs. Two recent
  papers deal with SRDB's spatial clustering by sample weighting (Jiang 2025 AFM; title in Crossref,
  abstract read by the agent) or representativeness pruning (Cao 2026 GCB; abstract checked in
  Crossref: a 1,363-site subset lowers global Rs from 100.5 to 94.1 Pg C/yr), not by geographic
  hold-out. Both must be cited, and they undercut any claim to be first to show that sampling bias
  distorts machine-learning Rs estimates. Jiang 2025's reference list cites spatial
  cross-validation methods and Gomes 2026's cites the area-of-applicability literature, so either
  may use spatial folds [UNVERIFIED; full texts blocked]. Spatial CV exists for laboratory Q10
  (Saez-Sandino 2026), not for field Rs.
- **A2.** No study found that uses replicated annual Rs records within a grid cell (or site) to bound
  explainable variance. SRDB-V5 (read in full) partitions no variance. Jian 2018 (abstract: the
  "sources of variability" are upscaling assumptions such as model form and data time step) and
  Bond-Lamberty & Thomson 2010 (abstract says it "briefly" examines Rs variability) could not be read
  in full [UNVERIFIED]. Closest precedents: Bahn 2010 (cross-site prediction error 14-51% vs
  within-site uncertainty of annual Rs 9-25%, 57 sites, not SRDB), Rammig 2018 (within-pixel
  variability and observation error in DGVM-plot comparisons; abstract only), replicate eddy-
  covariance towers (Oren 2006, Hill 2017), generic noise ceilings in other fields (Li 2021,
  Lage-Castellanos 2019), Hengl 2017 (SoilGrids residual variograms pure nugget below 300 km), Cai
  2023 and Giasson 2013 for within-site Rs variability. Reported random-split R2 for SRDB machine
  learning (0.76-0.79; Laffitte 2026, Liu J 2024) exceed the site-level ceiling here, which suggests
  but does not prove leakage through replicated sites (targets differ).
  Refuter: the claim survives only in its soil-respiration form and is not safe as a claim of
  methodological novelty. The same idea, correcting skill for noise in replicate ground data,
  exists for satellite estimates of yields and household outcomes (Lobell 2021; abstract checked in
  Crossref: with noise accounted for, satellite r2 is higher and occasionally doubles) and for DGVM
  pixel-to-plot comparisons (Rammig 2018, abstract only). Bahn 2010 (full text) remains the
  closest Rs precedent. Its within-site benchmark comes from the literature, its main predictor is
  measured at the site, and it computes no R2 bound. Within-site noise without a skill bound:
  Lavoie 2015 (abstract checked in Crossref: random error about 40% and spatial scaling error about
  50% of mean Rs), Cai 2023, Giasson 2013, Smykalov 2026. Full texts with no ceiling: Sun 2023 (10-fold
  CV R2 0.69, as reported by the agent), Ciais 2021, Huang 2020, Laffitte 2026. Methods or
  supplements were blocked for Warner 2019, Stell 2021, Hashimoto 2015, Konings 2019, Yao 2021, He
  2022, Lu 2021 and Jian 2018, so a ceiling-like passage there cannot be excluded [UNVERIFIED].
  Wording to use: "To our knowledge, no published soil-respiration upscaling study has used
  replicate annual Rs records sharing a grid cell or site to estimate a maximum attainable R2 for
  models driven by gridded predictors", citing Bahn 2010, Rammig 2018 and Lobell 2021.
- **A3.** The method is not new: country-mismatch, sea and sign/swap tests are standard in
  biodiversity data cleaning (Yesson 2007; CoordinateCleaner, Zizka 2019; biogeo, Robertson 2016;
  bdc, Ribeiro 2022; GBIF flags) and have been applied to other ecological databases (Tallo, Jucker
  2022; GlobalRx, Hsu 2025). SRDB's history shows piecemeal fixes (51 longitude sign flips and 38
  lat/lon swaps across the git history; ORNL's 104 records in 2015, issue #2), no erratum for either
  SRDB paper, and no published paper reporting SRDB or COSORE coordinate errors. A systematic
  country-consistency audit of SRDB and a measurement of its effect on Rs upscaling appear new.
